1. Stance & Crease Movement

At 12 years old, dynamic movement inside the crease is your primary tool. You must arrive at your spot before the shooter is ready to release the puck.

Key Techniques to Master:

  • Shuffles: Used for short distance adjustments when the puck moves slowly across the ice.
  • T-Pushes: Used for rapid, long-distance movements across the crease during cross-ice passes.
  • Butterfly Slides: Sliding on your pads to cover the bottom net angle on low rebound plays.

2. Angles, Depth & Tracking

Good goaltending isn't about making flashy saves; it's about making saves look easy through position and tracking.

Core Principles:

  • Angle: Stay lined up centered between the puck and the middle of your net.
  • Depth: Challenge at the top of your crease on clear shots; pull back toward the posts as the play gets closer.
  • Eye Tracking: Keep your eyes glued on the puck from the stick blade all the way into your body, glove, or pads.

3. Playing the Puck & Breakouts

Goalies are the third defenseman on the ice! Stopping rimmed pucks and setting up breakouts helps our team transition to attack faster.

What to master:

  • Stopping dump-ins cleanly behind the net for your defensemen.
  • Making quick, confident breakout passes when defenders are under pressure.
  • When to play the puck vs. when to freeze it

4. On-Ice Communication

You see the entire ice! Your vocal cues help defensemen make faster decisions under pressure during breakouts and D-zone coverage.

Essential Goalie Calls:

  • "RIMS!" – Alert your defensemen that a puck is being dumped around the boards.
  • "TIME!" – Let your defenseman know they have room to turn and control the puck.
  • "MAN ON!" – Warn your defenseman that an opponent is coming quickly to forecheck them.
  • "OVER!" / "REVERSE!" – Directing your partner where to transfer the puck behind the net.

Stance & Recovery Checklist

  • 🏒

    Stick Position: 3-4 inches in front of skate toes, flat on the ice.

  • 👁️

    Head & Eyes: Lead with your eyes first, then your stick/hands, then your feet.

  • 📐

    Glove & Blocker: Hands held forward out in front of body, elbows clear of torso.

  • 🔄

    Butterfly Recovery: Always recover with the lead foot nearest to where the puck is moving.

When to Play vs. Freeze the Puck

PLAY the Puck when:

  • You can stop a rimmed puck behind the net safely.
  • Your defenseman is under heavy forecheck pressure and needs an easy outlet.
  • The puck is loose in the crease with no forecheckers nearby.

FREEZE the Puck when:

  • Your team has been stuck on ice for a long shift and needs a whistle.
  • An opponent is bearing down hard on you in the crease.
  • The puck is dangerous/loose in high-traffic slot areas.

Quick Decision Formula

  1. 1 Can I safely stop or pass the puck? → Play It
  2. 2 Is there immediate opponent pressure? → Cover / Freeze
  3. 3 Are your teammates tired? → Freeze for Whistle
